# AI-Assisted Ticket Buying and Fair Allocation

COMSCI/ECON 206 Problem Set 2

## Project Overview

This project studies how AI-assisted ticket-buying tools may change the allocation of limited concert tickets.

Traditional ticket purchasing requires time and effort. Genuine fans who highly value attending a concert may therefore be willing to spend more time waiting, refreshing pages, and completing purchases.

AI-assisted tools can reduce this participation cost by automating parts of the ticket-buying process. This may allow resale-oriented buyers or scalpers to participate at larger scale and compete more effectively for limited tickets.

Our project asks how ticket-allocation mechanisms should change when AI reduces the cost of strategic participation.

## Shared Research Question

How does AI-assisted ticket buying change the allocation of limited concert tickets between genuine fans and scalpers, and which allocation mechanism better protects genuine access and social welfare?

## Participants

We model two types of ticket buyers:

### Genuine Fans

Genuine fans receive value from actually attending the concert. They may be willing to spend time and effort purchasing tickets because the concert itself has high value to them.

### Scalpers

Scalpers primarily value tickets for their resale opportunities. Their profit depends on obtaining tickets and reselling them at a higher price.

AI-assisted tools reduce the time and effort required to participate, which may allow scalpers to enter more aggressively or at larger scale.

## Role of AI

AI is modeled as a reduction in participation cost.

Without AI, entering and competing for tickets requires substantial manual effort.

With AI-assisted tools, this effort cost is lower.

The main causal relationship studied in this project is:

AI assistance
→ lower participation cost
→ increased strategic participation
→ different ticket allocation
→ changes in welfare and genuine-fan access

## Allocation Mechanisms

We compare two ticket-allocation mechanisms.

### Mechanism 1: First-Come, First-Served (FCFS)

Tickets are allocated to participants who complete the purchase process first.

Speed therefore directly affects allocation.

### Mechanism 2: Randomized Queue

Participants who successfully enter during a defined purchase window are randomly ordered before tickets are allocated.

This reduces the direct advantage of completing the process slightly faster.

## Experimental Design

We compare four conditions:

1. FCFS without AI assistance
2. FCFS with AI assistance
3. Randomized Queue without AI assistance
4. Randomized Queue with AI assistance

This 2 × 2 design allows us to study both:

- the effect of AI-assisted participation;
- the effect of the allocation mechanism.

## Main Metrics

We evaluate each condition using four main measures.

### Genuine-Fan Allocation Rate

The proportion of available tickets allocated to genuine fans.

### Scalper Allocation Rate

The proportion of available tickets allocated to scalpers.

### Total Welfare

The total value generated by the final ticket allocation minus participation costs.

### Scalper Profit

The resale profit earned by scalpers minus their participation costs.

## Strategic Model

Participants decide whether and how strongly to compete for tickets based on their expected benefit and participation cost.

AI assistance changes the strategic environment because it lowers participation costs, particularly for participants who attempt to acquire tickets at scale.

We test whether changing the allocation mechanism can reduce the resulting advantage.

## Computational Method

We use Python in Google Colab to simulate repeated ticket-allocation environments.

The main libraries are:

- NumPy
- pandas
- Matplotlib

The simulation does not require a GPU.

A fixed random seed will be used where appropriate so that the main results can be reproduced.

## Planned Computational Comparison

The baseline comparison is:

FCFS without AI
→ FCFS with AI

We then test the mechanism intervention:

FCFS
→ Randomized Queue

under both AI and non-AI conditions.

The main question is whether the Randomized Queue reduces the increase in scalper allocation caused by AI-assisted participation.

## Evidence Boundary

The computational model uses simulated participants and synthetic parameters.

The results therefore show how the stated model behaves under the specified assumptions. They do not establish the behavior of real ticket buyers or real ticketing platforms.

Behavioral evidence collected through classroom or peer interaction will be treated as exploratory rather than population-level evidence.
